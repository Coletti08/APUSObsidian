Key Aspects of the Compromise Tariff:
    
    The bill provided for a gradual reduction of tariffs over a ten-year period, starting in 1833 and ending in 1842. 
    
    **Resolution of the Nullification Crisis:**
    
    The compromise was intended to defuse the Nullification Crisis, which had seen South Carolina threaten secession due to its dissatisfaction with high tariffs. 
    
    
    South Carolina ultimately repealed its Ordinance of Nullification after the passage of the Compromise Tariff. 
    

Historical Context:

- The Compromise Tariff followed the passage of the [[Tariff of 1832]], which had been enacted to appease the South but was still perceived as unfavorable by some Southern states.

- The [[Nullification Crisis]] stemmed from a belief that high tariffs favored Northern industries at the expense of Southern farmers and planters.

- The compromise was a delicate balancing act between the desire to address Southern concerns and the need to maintain a strong federal government. 

Long-Term Significance:

- While the Compromise Tariff successfully resolved the immediate crisis, it also highlighted the deep-seated sectional differences and tensions that would eventually lead to the Civil War.

- The Nullification Crisis and the Compromise Tariff set a precedent for future debates about states' rights and federal authority.
