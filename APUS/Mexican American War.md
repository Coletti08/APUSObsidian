
- In 1846 [[James K Polk]] sent a US representative to Mexican government to make an offer to buy California and parts of New Mexico as well as to settle disputed territory claims in Texas. 

- In exchange for this land he offered 35-30 million and an additional 3 million in debt relief owed to American citizens by Mexico. 

- After the government refused to meet with the representatives Polk ordered the US army move into the disputed territory. which lead to conflict on April 25. 

- The people fighting in the Mexican American war disliked the annexation of Texas in 1845, and the [[Texas Declaration of Independence]]. 

- How did the Mexican American War influence the beginning of the [[Civil War]]:
	
	- [[American Expansion]] gained a vast amount of land from Mexico through the [[Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo]] (California, Arizona, New Mexico, Utah, Nevada, etc.).
	    
	- This raised the critical question: **Would slavery be allowed in the new territories?**
	    
	    - The **Wilmot Proviso** (1846) attempted to ban slavery in any territory taken from Mexico—it failed but **deepened the North-South divide**.
	
	
	- It fractured national political parties (especially the [[Democrats]] and [[Whigs]]) and **led to the rise of the [[Republican]] Party** in the 1850s, which opposed the expansion of slavery.
	    
	
	- Many **future Civil War generals** fought in the Mexican-American War, including:
	    
	    - [[Ulysses S. Grant]] (Union)
	        
	    - [[Robert E. Lee]] (Confederacy)
	        
	    - **Stonewall Jackson**, **George McClellan**, and others
	        
	- They gained **valuable battlefield experience**, which they carried into leadership roles during the Civil War.
	
	
	- The war **intensified distrust** between North and South.
	    
	- Many Northerners saw it as a **"slaveholders’ war"** to expand Southern power, while Southerners saw it as an opportunity to expand their economic system.
