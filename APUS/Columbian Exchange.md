
- The discovery of the [[New World]], and the trade flow between the Americas, Europe, and Africa became known as the [Columbian Exchange]
- People, animals, plants, and disease passed from continent to continent affecting virtually all aspects of life for the people living in these places. 

- The Columbian exchange was built upon four distinct things:
    
- Plants:
    
    The Americas introduced crops like potatoes, maize, tomatoes, and beans to the Old World, which became staples in many diets and fueled population growth. Conversely, the Old World brought crops like wheat, rice, sugar, and citrus fruits to the Americas, impacting agriculture and food availability.
    
- Animals:
    
    The Old World introduced domesticated animals like horses, pigs, cattle, and sheep to the Americas, which revolutionized transportation and food production in some areas. The Americas, in turn, introduced animals like turkeys to the Old World.
    
- Diseases:
    
    The Old World introduced diseases like smallpox, measles, and influenza to the Americas, leading to devastating population losses among Indigenous populations who lacked immunity. The Americas, in turn, introduced diseases like syphilis to the Old World.
    
- Human Populations:
    
    The exchange also involved the movement of people, including European colonists, and the forced migration of enslaved Africans to the Americas. These migrations had a profound impact on the demographics and cultural landscapes of both regions.
