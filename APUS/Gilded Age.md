
The **Gilded Age** (roughly **1870 to 1900**) was a period in American history marked by **rapid economic growth**, **industrialization**, and **urbanization**—but also by **extreme inequality**, **political corruption**, and **social unrest**. The term was coined by **Mark Twain**, suggesting that the era was glittering on the surface but corrupt and troubled underneath.

---

### Key Features of the Gilded Age:

#### 1. **Industrial and Economic Expansion**

- Massive growth in **railroads, steel, oil, and manufacturing**
    
- Rise of powerful industrialists like [[Andrew Carnegie]] (steel)**, [[John Rockefeller]] (oil)**, and [[J.P. Morgan]] (finance)**
    
- Growth of national markets and **corporate monopolies (“trusts”)**
    

#### 2. **Urbanization and Immigration**

- Millions of immigrants, especially from **Southern and Eastern Europe**, arrived seeking jobs
    
- Cities expanded rapidly, often without proper infrastructure—leading to **overcrowded tenements and poor sanitation**
    

#### 3. **Wealth Inequality**

- A few amassed enormous wealth (“**robber barons**”), while many workers faced **low wages**, **long hours**, and **unsafe conditions**
    
- The divide between rich and poor fueled **labor strikes** and **populist movements**
    

#### 4. **Labor Unrest and Reform**

- Major labor events like the [[Great Railway Strike of 1877]] (1877)**, [[The Haymarket Square Riot]] (1886)**, and **[[Pullman Strike]] (1894)**
    
- Rise of unions like the **Knights of Labor** and the **American Federation of Labor (AFL)**
    

#### 5. **Political Corruption and Machine Politics**

- Politics dominated by **patronage and corruption** (e.g., **Tammany Hall** in New York)
    
- Presidents during this era were often seen as weak or ineffective
    
- Reforms like the **Pendleton Civil Service Act (1883)** began to challenge the **spoils system**
    

#### 6. **Populist and Agrarian Movements**

- **Farmers’ Alliances** and the [[Populist Party]] (People’s Party) arose to fight corporate power, call for **bimetallism**, and demand reforms
