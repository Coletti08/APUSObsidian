Key aspects of the Whiskey Rebellion:

- **Whiskey Tax
    The tax was imposed in 1791 to generate revenue for the new nation, but it disproportionately affected small-scale frontier distillers who relied on whiskey as a means of livelihood and currency. 
    
- **Resistance and Violence:**
    
    Farmers in western Pennsylvania initially resisted the tax by refusing to pay, and then resorted to more aggressive tactics, including tarring and feathering tax collectors and attacking their homes. 
    
- **Escalation and Proclamation:**
    
    The federal government responded with increased enforcement efforts, leading to the formation of a large rebel force. President Washington issued a proclamation on August 7, 1794, authorizing Secretary of the Treasury Alexander Hamilton to organize troops to quell the rebellion. 
    
- [[George Washington]]'s Response:**
    
    Washington personally led 12,950 soldiers into western Pennsylvania, demonstrating the federal government's resolve to enforce its laws. This was the first and only time a sitting U.S. president led troops in an active campaign. 
    
- **Outcome and Pardons:**
    
    The rebellion was quickly suppressed, and several leaders were arrested and charged with treason. However, the trials were hampered by a lack of evidence, and Washington ultimately pardoned the two convicted men, according to Mount Vernon.
