1. Defining the "New World":

- **Geographical Location:**
    
    The term primarily encompasses the continents of North and South America, along with surrounding islands. 
    
- **Historical Context:**
    
    The term emerged in the late 15th century, with [Christopher Columbus](https://www.google.com/search?client=opera-gx&sca_esv=c2417ebeb73254dc&cs=1&sxsrf=AHTn8zoYWVkamjw49cOmm63geyRIE6LaBA%3A1746034194186&q=Christopher+Columbus&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwjP9taepICNAxVRMlkFHbVbK-YQxccNegQIHRAB&mstk=AUtExfA9j7noFCyaSOsgHnAnfefTcOkHYGdTgRTvWvNltSBunucX3ba4tkcFG4Va6fcgGM6AwAmMfkohmVhA8mKzLd2DBvEhpZtgtn6Um3ygRYrMq7RYKyVxb9tHs4D93s3CGH2Pk1r6hkL1tUf2m4KYvnXB4BhDZWx69cXp2pPFDjCH9rtXWabfUQwQGxn0jyptRHbLTduCA5aYPnVX1P9iHZJOAVBYqRsnc7JVsbU9fppjlqjgZ_PDvR2AejvK9g-GFKGfELUKOGu2bDI389IYIEyw&csui=3)'s voyages to the Americas in 1492 being a key event. 
    
- **European Perspective:**
    
    From a European perspective, the Americas were considered "new" because they were largely unknown to Europeans before the Age of Exploration. 
    

1. Key Events and Impacts:

- **Exploration and Colonization:**
    
    European explorers, such as Columbus, [Amerigo Vespucci](https://www.google.com/search?client=opera-gx&sca_esv=c2417ebeb73254dc&cs=1&sxsrf=AHTn8zoYWVkamjw49cOmm63geyRIE6LaBA%3A1746034194186&q=Amerigo+Vespucci&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwjP9taepICNAxVRMlkFHbVbK-YQxccNegQIPBAB&mstk=AUtExfA9j7noFCyaSOsgHnAnfefTcOkHYGdTgRTvWvNltSBunucX3ba4tkcFG4Va6fcgGM6AwAmMfkohmVhA8mKzLd2DBvEhpZtgtn6Um3ygRYrMq7RYKyVxb9tHs4D93s3CGH2Pk1r6hkL1tUf2m4KYvnXB4BhDZWx69cXp2pPFDjCH9rtXWabfUQwQGxn0jyptRHbLTduCA5aYPnVX1P9iHZJOAVBYqRsnc7JVsbU9fppjlqjgZ_PDvR2AejvK9g-GFKGfELUKOGu2bDI389IYIEyw&csui=3), and others, initiated voyages to the Americas, leading to exploration and colonization efforts. 
    
- **Cultural Exchange ([Columbian Exchange](https://www.google.com/search?client=opera-gx&sca_esv=c2417ebeb73254dc&cs=1&sxsrf=AHTn8zoYWVkamjw49cOmm63geyRIE6LaBA%3A1746034194186&q=Columbian+Exchange&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwjP9taepICNAxVRMlkFHbVbK-YQxccNegQILhAB&mstk=AUtExfA9j7noFCyaSOsgHnAnfefTcOkHYGdTgRTvWvNltSBunucX3ba4tkcFG4Va6fcgGM6AwAmMfkohmVhA8mKzLd2DBvEhpZtgtn6Um3ygRYrMq7RYKyVxb9tHs4D93s3CGH2Pk1r6hkL1tUf2m4KYvnXB4BhDZWx69cXp2pPFDjCH9rtXWabfUQwQGxn0jyptRHbLTduCA5aYPnVX1P9iHZJOAVBYqRsnc7JVsbU9fppjlqjgZ_PDvR2AejvK9g-GFKGfELUKOGu2bDI389IYIEyw&csui=3)):**
    
    The interaction between Europeans and Native Americans resulted in the exchange of goods, ideas, plants, animals, and diseases, profoundly impacting both societies. 
    
- **Social and Economic Transformations:**
    
    European colonization led to significant changes in the social, economic, and political landscape of the Americas. 
    
- **Rise of European Empires:**
    
    The wealth and resources gained from the Americas contributed to the rise of European empires, particularly Spain and Portugal. 
    

1. "New World" vs. "Old World":

- **Distinction:**
    
    The terms "New World" and "Old World" are used to contrast the Americas with the continents of Europe, Asia, and Africa, which were considered to have a longer history of human settlement and interaction.
    
- **Focus of Study:**
    
    In academic settings, the study of history often involves a division between Old World history (focusing on Europe, Asia, and Africa) and New World history (focusing on the Americas).
