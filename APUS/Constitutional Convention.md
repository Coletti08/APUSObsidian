- **Purpose:**
    
    The convention was called to address the flaws of the Articles of Confederation, which proved inadequate in governing the newly independent nation. 
    

- **Location:**
    
    The convention took place in Philadelphia, specifically at the State House (later Independence Hall). 
    

- **Delegates:**
    
    Delegates from 12 states (excluding Rhode Island) attended, including prominent figures like [George Washington], [James Madison], [Benjamin Franklin], and others. 
    

- **Process:**
    
    Initially intended to amend the Articles, the convention quickly decided to create a new, stronger government structure. Delegates debated and compromised on issues like representation, the power of the federal government, and the role of the executive and judicial branches. 
    

- **Outcomes:**
    
    The convention produced[[ The Constitution]], a document that established a federal government with a system of checks and balances, and defined the rights and responsibilities of citizens. 
    

- **Ratification:**
    
    The Constitution was then sent to the states for ratification, and it took effect after it was ratified by nine states. 
    

Key issues addressed and compromises reached:

- **Representation:**
    
    The debate over representation in the legislature, particularly between large and small states, led to the [Great Compromise](https://www.google.com/search?client=opera-gx&hs=gwL&sca_esv=85ac0d99a7443db9&sxsrf=AHTn8zo-YY0cu-F6qfovTq8JNuq47Tt0wA%3A1745898171079&q=Great+Compromise&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwjrn7TCqfyMAxW8FFkFHVxwAykQxccNegUI-gEQAQ&mstk=AUtExfCRK2zhcXVNUrrRH_kH6YRYlQOkmloSKtYpSxu3k32O2l0dcufwPx1am4j5ND3Ni4ZH3K9A5O3iROu0mA5cU54s1FrKhYanfHZlTzjOnrcLEJUfw-SWcSlLklQlvuwhxc8bsU1jzTl_R3AshmSkC2t8uOacs8PWsf4oPaNLpkOaFbW5fMlxcJyMqjL0MmSnoKjKgPF1vJIfiC73pRzgkQUYUhwqN0KZ8U6PgHmAetvqN8t1mnmZTAZ62F8HPNboIOK2Tz6LSacRtQweB6wty0az&csui=3) (also known as the Connecticut Compromise), which created a bicameral legislature with the House of Representatives based on population and the Senate with equal representation for each state. 
    

- **Slavery:**
    
    The issue of slavery was a contentious point, and the delegates reached the [Three-Fifths Compromise](https://www.google.com/search?client=opera-gx&hs=gwL&sca_esv=85ac0d99a7443db9&sxsrf=AHTn8zo-YY0cu-F6qfovTq8JNuq47Tt0wA%3A1745898171079&q=Three-Fifths+Compromise&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwjrn7TCqfyMAxW8FFkFHVxwAykQxccNegUI_AEQAQ&mstk=AUtExfCRK2zhcXVNUrrRH_kH6YRYlQOkmloSKtYpSxu3k32O2l0dcufwPx1am4j5ND3Ni4ZH3K9A5O3iROu0mA5cU54s1FrKhYanfHZlTzjOnrcLEJUfw-SWcSlLklQlvuwhxc8bsU1jzTl_R3AshmSkC2t8uOacs8PWsf4oPaNLpkOaFbW5fMlxcJyMqjL0MmSnoKjKgPF1vJIfiC73pRzgkQUYUhwqN0KZ8U6PgHmAetvqN8t1mnmZTAZ62F8HPNboIOK2Tz6LSacRtQweB6wty0az&csui=3), which counted slaves as three-fifths of a person for the purpose of population representation in the House of Representatives. 
    

- **Executive Power:**
    
    The power of the executive branch was debated, with some favoring a single executive and others a committee of executives. The delegates ultimately agreed on a single executive, the President, with specific powers and responsibilities. 
    

- **Separation of Powers:**
    
    The Constitution established a system of separation of powers, dividing the federal government into legislative, executive, and judicial branches, with checks and balances to prevent any one branch from becoming too powerful.
