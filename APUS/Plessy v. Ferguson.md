
- In the wake of the 1890 [[Separate Car Act]], requiring blacks and white patrons to sit in separate sections of railcars on interstate railroads. Plessy, in a pre-meditated act of defiance, boarded a train to new Orleans and announced that he was neithor white nor inclined to move to a section for African Americans. He was arrested and convicted of violating the act. He appealed his conviction all the way to the US Supreme court, which ruled that as long as the accommodations were "equal", states could legally enforce segregation, 

- This was a landmark setback for [[Civil Rights]] movements of during the [[Gilded Age]], and set the president for more oppressive [[Jim Crow]] laws in both the south and the north.


- Plessy v. Ferguson stood until another Supreme Court verdict in 1954, [[Brown v. Board]].
