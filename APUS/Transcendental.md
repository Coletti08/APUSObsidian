
- Mainly consisting of well educated Northern men such as: Ralf Waldo Emerson, Henry David Thoreau , Bronson Alcott, Margaret Fuller. 

- Henry David Thoreau's essay "Civil Disobedience" encouraged people to speak out against the things they didn't believe in, and that everyone had the right to protest against the Civil Government.  
	- Thoreau's essay directly engages the debate on slavery being heavily pro abolition. 
	- the core of "Civil Disobedience" is:
		-  Conscience Over Law
			- Thoreau claims that individuals should not blindly follow government laws if those laws violate their moral principles.
		- Opposition to Slavery
			- Thoreau was a staunch abolitionist. He viewed slavery as a **profound moral evil** and criticized the U.S. government for supporting it—especially through:
			- The [[Fugitive Slave Act]], which forced citizens in free states to return escaped slaves
			- The [[Mexican American War]], which he believed was fought to expand slave territory
		- Refusal to Pay Taxes:
		- Thoreau practiced what he preached—he refused to pay a poll tax because he believed it supported a government that upheld slavery and war.  
- Thoreau was briefly jailed for this act, which became the foundation of _Civil Disobedience_.
