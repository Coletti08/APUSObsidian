 **Transportation Enabled the Market Revolution**

- New travel infrastructure—**roads, canals, steamboats, and later railroads**—dramatically **reduced the cost and time** of shipping goods and moving people.
    
- This allowed **farmers, manufacturers, and merchants** to reach **broader markets**, not just local ones.
    
- Example: The **Erie Canal (completed in 1825)** linked western farms with eastern cities, fueling commercial growth in places like New York City.
    

- **Market Demands Drove Transportation Innovation**

- As the national economy expanded and demand for goods grew, the need for **faster and more reliable transportation** rose.

- Investment in infrastructure became essential to maintain economic momentum, leading to **federal and state-sponsored projects**.
