
- There is a stack contrast between the [[Gilded Age]] and the [[Progressive Movement]], you could even make an argument that many of the failed reforms  proposed during the Gilded age are the back bone of the progressive movement.

- [[Teddy Roosevelt]] decides that trusts aren't inherently bad, but they commit abuses, so he begins [[Trust Busting]]. 

- The progressive movement in essence successfully removes power from big business, and returns it to the people, which makes many things such as [[labor unions]], and strikes far more successful going forward. 

- The progressive movement was spawned after the [[Panic of 1893]], with people concerned about maintaining America's economic stability. 

- the progressives wanted to address:
	- the Growing power of Big Businesses
	- Uncertainties in the economy
	- increasingly violent conflicts between labor groups and their employers
	- political machine power
	- Jim Crow segregation in the South
	- lack of Women's suffrage -- [[19th Amendment]]
	- and Alcohol [[Prohibition]] -- [[18th Amendment]]

- They disagreed on may things. However, they all agreed that: society, on some level, was deteriorating and the only cure was significant government intervention. 
	- That true societal change wouldn't occur purely due to "grass root efforts".
	- That the government had to get involved in a vigorous and invasive manner. 

- The start of the progressive movement gave rise to people determined to rake the muck of American industrial life. [[Muckraking]]. 
	- Upton Sinclair -- [[The Jungle]]
	- Ida Tarbell  -- [[John Rockefeller]]'s terrible [[Standard Oil Company]]
	- Jacob Riis -- [["How the Other Half Lives"]]
	

- The progressives wanted to put the power of voting back in the hands of the people, and they did this through the [[Secrete Ballot]].
	- They also pushed for the [[Direct Election of Senators]] done through the [17th Amendment]
