
- Slave Revolution in San Domingue (Haiti) sparked by the 1791 French Revolution. 
- Second document outlining independence. (After America). Emerged in a very different context then the American Independence. 
	- All the other Major powers of the world relied on slavery, so the Haitian revolution needed to be far more violent to show that they are powerful. 
- Was inspired by the [[American Revolution]] to break free from imperial rule.
- No countries acknowledged the Haitian revolution until 1825, when Haiti payed France indemnities. 

- The number of slaves imported to San Domingue were extremely high. However, the death rate equaled the import rate. 


- **Slave Revolt
    Enslaved people in Saint-Domingue, inspired by the French Revolution, launched a major uprising against their French masters. 
    
- **French Colonization:**
    
    The colony was incredibly prosperous, producing vast amounts of sugar, coffee, and other goods, all thanks to a forced labor system. 
    

- **Toussaint L'Ouverture:**
    
    A former slave who rose to become a skilled military leader, he played a crucial role in organizing the revolution and ultimately leading the Haitian forces to victory. 
    

- **Jean-Jacques Dessalines:**
    
    Another key figure, he succeeded Toussaint after his capture and proclaimed Haiti's independence in 1804. 
    

- **International Involvement:**
    
    The revolution saw involvement from France, Britain, Spain, and even some North American powers, each with their own interests and strategies. 
    

- **Impact:**
    
    The revolution not only freed enslaved people in Haiti but also had a global impact, influencing abolitionist movements and sparking fears among slaveholding nations in the Americas. 
    

- **Legacy:**
    
    Haiti's independence was followed by a period of hardship and economic challenges, including being forced to pay reparations to France for the loss of its "property" (i.e., enslaved people), which continue to have a lasting impact on Haitian society.
