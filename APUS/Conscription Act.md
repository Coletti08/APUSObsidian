

- The act that created the [Federal Draft].

- The **Conscription Act of 1863**, also known as the **Enrollment Act**, was the **first [federal draft] law** in United States history. Passed during the [[Civil War]], it allowed the **[[Union]] government to draft men into military service** due to declining volunteer numbers.

    Both the Union and Confederate governments enacted conscription acts during the U.S. Civil War. The Union's act, passed in 1863, required registration for all male citizens and immigrants who had applied for citizenship between the ages of 20 and 45. The Confederacy's first conscription act, in 1862, made all white males between 18 and 35 liable for military service. 
    
    
    The [[Selective Service Act]] of 1917 authorized the U.S. government to temporarily expand the military through conscription, requiring all men between 21 and 45 to register. 
    
    
    The Selective Training and Service Act of 1940, also known as the [[Burke–Wadsworth Act]], established the first peacetime conscription in U.S. history, initially targeting men between 21 and 35. 
    

Key Provisions:

- **Registration:**
    
    Conscription laws typically require individuals within a specified age range to register with draft boards. 
    

- **Draft Lottery:**
    
    A lottery system is often used to randomly select individuals for military service from those who have registered. 
    

- **Exemptions and Substitutions:**
    
    Some conscription laws allow for exemptions based on medical conditions, personal circumstances (like being the sole support of a family), or other reasons. In some cases, individuals may be able to hire a substitute to fulfill their military obligation, or pay a commutation fee. 
    

- **Enforcement:**
    
    Enforcement of conscription laws is typically handled by government agencies and military authorities.
