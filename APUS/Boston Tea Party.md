- **Background:**
    
    The colonists felt that the Tea Act was unfair because it allowed the East India Company to sell tea without paying the same taxes as colonial merchants. They also resented being taxed without representation in Parliament. 
    

- **The Protest:**
    
    On December 16, 1773, members of the Sons of Liberty, a group of colonial patriots, boarded three ships carrying tea in Boston Harbor and dumped the tea into the sea. 
    

- **Significance:**
    
    The Boston Tea Party became a powerful symbol of colonial resistance to British rule and is considered a major event leading up to the [[American Revolution]]. It demonstrated the colonists' determination to resist unfair taxation and the lack of representation. 
    

- **Consequences:**
    
    The British government responded to the Boston Tea Party by enacting the [[The Intolerable Acts]], also known as the Intolerable Acts, which imposed further restrictions on Massachusetts and further escalated tensions with the colonies.
