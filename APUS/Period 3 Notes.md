- Key events and information:
    

- The Seven Years’ War (French and Indian War 1755)
    

- Fought along the frontier between New France and the British colonies. 
    
- Lasted from 1754 to 1763.
    
- Britain claimed they had stake over the east coast because they were “there the longest”
    
- Lead to an intense national feeling WITH Britain, they epitomize all the good qualities of the british. (fades quickly after this).
    
-   
    

- The proclamation of 1763:
    

- At the end of the Seven Years War
    
- France surrendered Canada and much of the Ohip and Mississippi river Valleys to England. 
    
- *Proclamation decrees that all land west of the Appalachian Mountains were off limits to white settlers. 
    
- restricted commerce with American Indians and traders licensed by the British. 
    
- The British government hoped that by restricting the settlers freedom to move around they would get into less costly wars, and protect the fur trade.
    
- To verify that their demands were met, the British sent 10,000 troops along the frontier. 
    

- The American Revolution:
    

- The Bloody Massacre(1770) / Boston Massacre:
    

- The British soldier presence in Boston rose steadily, and with it so did tensions between American and the British. 
    
- March 5, 1770 crowds of day laborers, apprentices, and merchant sailors began to pelt British soldiers with snowballs and rocks. 
    
- The British fired upon the crowd, killing five civilians. 
    

- Unruly Americans in the Revolution:
    

- Taxes – but also Territory:
    

- “No taxation without representation”-- classic. 
    
- George Washington buying land beyond the Appalachian mountains
    
- Not being able to use or sell that land
    
- Revolution could fix that issue. 
    

- Indispensable Allies:
    

- As British taxation on imported goods, such as textiles and beverages, American patriots began to boycott these items.

- The American Revolution, 1763 - 1783:
    

- Much hope for peace after the seven years war ended in 1763.
    
- Britain wanted the Colonists to help pay for the war. 
    
- This was made more difficult by Pontiac’s Rebellion
    
- Conflict grew with two acts:
    

- Stamp Act(1764):
    

- Levied new taxes (sayles tax) on pamphlets, almanacs, newspapers, playing cards, dice, and a wide range of documents. 
    
- This act was forced upon them with almost no colonial intervention or say. 
    
- August 14, 1765. Massachusetts patriots forced the stamp collector, Andrew Oliver to resign his office, effectively ending the stamp act in Massachusetts. 
    
- Enacted to pay for British troops stationed during the Seven Years’ War. 
    
- Required colonists to play a tax, represented by a stamp, on various forms of papers, documents, and playing cards. 
    
-   
    
- Repealed the Stamp act in the spring of 1766. 
    

- Sugar Act(1765):
    

- Made it more expensive for colonists to import molasses through non british channels. 
    
- Imposed certain duties on certain imports, not as in the past, to affect the course of grade. 
    

- Both acts violated Britain's own “no taxation without representation” decree under parliament, so Britain had no right to tax the colonists because they had no representatives in the house of commons. 
    

- American had “virtual representation”, but no “actual representation”, American patriots fought their virtual representation, demanding actual representation. 
    

- After the Stamp Act was repealed, Parliament passed the Declaratory Act (1766), which gave Britain the right to pass any laws they saw fit for the colonies. 
    
- In 1767 parliament levied new taxes on glass, paint, lead, paper, and tea imported into America. 
    

- All the other taxes were repealed, but the one on tea stuck. 
    

- 1773, the Tea Act was passed to help the financially strapped East India Company sell its surplus of tea in America, the Tea Act didn’t impose a new tax. 
    

- Gave the EIC a monopoly over American tea.
    

- Nov 28, 1773 first tea ship arrives in boston, 
    
- Dec 16, a group of men disguised as Indians threw 342 chests of tea into the harbor. 
    
- Fallout:
    

- Coercive Acts (intolerable Acts) 1773.
    

- Nullification of massachusetts charter of 1691
    
- Allowed royal officials accused of committing felonies while executing their offices in Massachusetts tried in england. 
    
- Quartering Act, facilitated housing troops where they could be used against colonial civilians. 
    

- Constitutional congress met in philadelphia on sept 5 - oct 26, 1774, and adapted the statement of rights, demanding the repeal of the Coercive Acts. 
    
- Parliament was not the focus of American disengagement, but the king. 
    

- The Declaration of Independence:
    

- The most radical idea created by American revolutionaries.
    
- “All men are created equal, that they are endowed by their creator with certain unalienable Rights, that among these are life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.” That is the American dream. 
    
- Peter Timothy was the first to print the declaration in the Gazette of South Carolina; he suspended publication from 1775 to 1777 in fear of British backlash. 
    
- Timothy was imprisoned in 1780 on charges of treason.

- The Articles of Confederation (Dec 16 1777):
    

- At the end of the conference a draft of the Articles of Confederation was sent to each state for ratification. 
    
- Virginia was first, Maryland was last. 
    
- Didn’t infringe upon the sovereignty of each of the 13 states, but looked to unionize them. 
    
- Under the articles states had the power to level taxes not congress. 
    
- Didn’t establish a genuinely republican government, power was concentrated in a single assembly, rather than being divided. As in, state governments, into houses and branches. (didn’t want to match the structure in Britain)
    
- The Articles were the plan of governorship until the US Constitution in 1788. 
    

- Shays’ Rebellion(1787):
    

- Daniel Shays and his insurrections confronted Massachusetts state militia forces on Jan 25, 1787. 
    
- Shays was a veteran, and a very poor farmer. Who was fed up with the large debts created by the revolution. 
    
- Washington was incredulous that so soon after US independence there could be such a virulent rebelion. 
    
- Washington chose not to attend the Constitutional Convention, but showed his support for the Knox plan for constructing a central national government. 
    

- The US Constitution (1788):
    

- Constitutional Convention September 17, 1787. 
    
- The Constitution was printed on March 21, 1788. 
    
- Struggle on government configuration between the Federalists and the Anti-Federalists:
    

- Believed in state sovereignty.
    
- At a steep disadvantage organizationally. 
    
- Knew the confederation wasn’t working (shays rebellion), but didn’t want as strong of a centralized government as the Constitution invoked. 
    
- Suggested that the Federalists were working in secret to undermine the American people. 
    
- Didn't create a bill of rights, because they used the states rights instead. This was a mistake, because the federalists used this to their advantage. 
    

Federalists:

- People who supported the articles of confederation. 
    
- Had been through the constitutional convention, and understood the debate structure much better then the Anti-Federalists. 
    
- Far more organized.
