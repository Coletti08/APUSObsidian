    **The Doctrine of Discovery:**
    
    The court relied on the [[Doctrine of Discovery]], a legal principle that justified European colonization by claiming that discovery gave them exclusive rights to lands not already governed by a Christian power. 
    

    **Indian Right of Occupancy:**
    
    The court acknowledged that Native Americans had a right of occupancy and use of the land, but not the right to sell the land to private individuals. 

    
    **Federal Government's Authority:**
    
    The court affirmed that the U.S. federal government had the exclusive right to acquire land from Native American tribes, and private citizens could only purchase land from the government. 
    
    
    **Land Dispute:**
    
    The case involved a dispute over land claims made by Johnson and his associates who had allegedly purchased land from the Piankeshaw Indians. McIntosh claimed ownership based on a later land grant from the United States government. 
    
    **Impact on Indian Law:**

    Johnson v. McIntosh is considered a foundational case in American Indian law, establishing the federal government's power over Indian lands and setting the stage for future legal battles over Native American land rights.
