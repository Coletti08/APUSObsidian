

- Key Concepts:
	1. Technological advancements, large-scale production methods, and the opening of new markets encouraged the rise of [industrial capitalism] in the United States. 

	2. The Migrations that accompanied industrialization transformed both urban and rural areas of the [[United States]] and caused dramatic social and cultural change. 

	3. The [[Gilded Age]] produces new cultural and intellectual movements, public reform efforts, and political debates over economic and social policies. 


- Focuses on the time from 1865 with the close of the civil war to 1898 as the nation prepared itself for the 20th century. 

- This time []()period marks the rapid change from agriculture to urban industrialization. 

- Themes:
	- Big Business:
		- In the years before 1900, America was transformed into the industrial power house of the 20th century:
			- American Cities grew at a rapid pace, urban centers exploded the growth of American cities parraled this rapid industrial development
			- These new hubs of industrial activities became the home of migrant workers
		- Regardless the community from which you originated life in American cities was not always easy, the new industrial life created deep divides between wealthy [[capitalists]] and poor workers. 
		- People like [[John Rockefeller]], or [[Andrew Carnegie]], [[J.P. Morgan]] 
		- The [[Dominant Factory System]] becomes an efficient manufacturing system that becomes part of the American economy that demands the maintenance of a permanent low-wage work force. 
			- An economy that operated free of federal and state governments.  
		- With all the wealth in the top 1%, and the lower classes of people making up the majority of the work force: people began to organize around issues of labor, [[Unions]] began to organize. 
		- Passage of Anti trust legislation gets rid of monopolies over parts of the market. 
			- [[Sherman Anti-Trust Act]] (1890)
		- After this Carnegie began his [[Gospel of Wealth]], which lead to his overwhelming phalntrhipy. 
			- [[The Haymarket Affair]]. 
	- Big cities:
		- Big cities and Big issues (Urbanization and immigration and social change):
		- Urban centers were ports for employment, so people flooded to them from all over the county making cities large economic powerhouses during this time.
			- [[New York City]] at this time became an economic powerhouse only at a global scale. 
		- Immigrants: New York City became a hub of immigration, the lower east side became the home of Italian and Jewish immigrants. 
			- This part of the city too became dirty and over crowded with no public transportation. 
			- newly immigrated people had to live in these poor dirty sectors of the city, because they 1. had no money, and 2. because everyone else like them already lived there. So everyone could provide support even if it was only moral. 
		- soon immigrants became nearly 15% of the total population. 
		- Urban centers too were the home of many reforms:
			- Women's rights became an integral part of the "American Dialog" during this time with the relevance of the [[Seneca Falls Movement]]. which became the national push towards [[Women's Suffrage]] in the 1920's. 
		- American society too began to turn towards the [[Temperance Movement]]:
			- For many this movement was seen as a way to purify a corrupt nation. 
		- The [[Chinese Exclusion Act]] of 1882, placed massive road blocks on Chinese immigration, and American culture too became very hostile towards Chinese immigrants. 
		- California's two largest cities get created by large industries as the [[California Gold Rush]] draws in mass amounts of people. 
		- the [[Purchase of Alaska]] in 1867 continues the American expansion westward. And the [[Trans Continental Railroad]] gets completed, which made the travel and communication across the country far easier. 
			- The [[Dawes Severalty Act]] of 1887, which was created to ease the Indian assimilation into mainstream society. 
		- in the "post [[Civil War]] south" [[Sharecropping]] became one of the only ways to survive as a newly freed black. 
			- Another theme of this [[new South]] is [[Jim Crow]]. Jim Crow was a set of un written rules that were established to make free blacks feel as if they were still in a pre-Civil War period. 
				- African American workers were beaten or even lynched if the came anywhere near the poles. 
			- This era saw the [[Plessy v. Ferguson]] case in 1896, where the judiciary interfered with interstate commerce when it established [[Separate but Equal]] as a viable scape goat for addressing free blacks. 
	- Big Changes:
		- New cultures emerged from this time period, due to the wealth gap, and the desire for low class workers to gain any sort of economic footing in a new country.
		- During the Gilded age, Civil Service changed. The [[Spoils System]] was abolished, and government work was given on the basis of merit. 
		- Currency issues became a heated debate weather the American currency should default to the [[Gold Standard]] or [[Silver Standard]]. 
		- Tariffs became a rising issue as well. As [[Globalization]] began to grip the economy, American needed to find a way to stay on top, and levying heavy taxes on incoming goods seemed a good way to acheive this.
		- Political and economic scandals such as the [[Credit Mobilier Scandal]] of 1872, showed the corrupt dealings of positions and members of the government accepting bribes from railroad billionaires. 
		- The [[Panic of 1893]] showed the country what real economic depression could be like. 
			- Because of this unemployment shot up, and life in America almost regressed for a time.
		- Because of these issues the [[Populist Movement]] began to gain traction. (Based in an Anti-Trust sentiment).
		- The [[Election of 1896]] becomes extremely important as [[William McKinley]] defeats [[William Jennings Bryan]], who made a lasting mark on US politics with his [[Cross of Gold]] speech. 


- European Immigration like today was a large problem during the [[Gilded Age]], more then 2,812,191 immigrants arrived in the united states between 1871 and 1880. 
	- These immigrant workers weren't inherently bad drawn to America by the large industrial capital allure of the large urban cities.
	- these immigrants were willing to be paid extremely low wages, and caused job suppression for average Americans
